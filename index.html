What to Expect When You Return

Even if the fire seemed small, your home may look very different now. Firefighters often have to break windows or cut holes in the roof to release dangerous heat and smoke. We also check for hidden fire inside walls, which may require removing sheetrock.

Please be careful: Soot and dirty water left behind can contain materials that may make you sick. It is best to minimize your time inside until it has been cleaned.

🛑 SAFETY FIRST

Do not enter your damaged home unless the Fire Department explicitly says it is safe.

  • Roofs and floors may be weakened.
  • Do not clean up or repair anything until the fire investigation is officially completed.
  • You may temporarily cover broken windows or holes to prevent weather damage, but do not disturb the scene.

Immediate Next Steps

🐾 Care for Your Pets

Scared animals often react by biting or scratching. Handle them gently.

Tip: Contact your vet to assess their overall health if they were exposed to smoke. Leave them with friends while handling logistics.

Animal Services: (940) 349-4898

🔒 Secure Your Property

If you must leave your home vacant, notify the police to prevent looting. You may need to board up windows/doors to secure the premises.

Call Denton PD (Non-Emergency) →

📞 Contact Your Insurance

Notify them immediately. Ask about "Loss of Use" coverage - this provides funds for hotels, food, and clothes.

Note: Ask for a cost estimate in writing from any restoration company before they begin work.

Rebuilding & Permits

The City of Denton requires a building permit to repair fire damage. We are here to help with the list of repairs and any required code updates. Updates like smoke detectors, carbon monoxide detectors, and wiring improvements will make your home safer. Please know that we are here to help.

Disaster / Fire Damage - cityofdenton.com →
Action 1 Call Building Safety: (940) 349-8600

The Process

  1. Request a courtesy fire inspection from Building Safety - (940) 349-8600.
  2. Full loss? You need a permit to demolish and a separate one to rebuild when ready.
    Demolition Permit → New Construction Permit →
  3. Review your repair list provided by the City with your contractor and insurance adjuster.
  4. Submit a building permit application with repair details and floor plans. You or your contractor can meet with a plans examiner for guidance.
    Residential Additions or Alterations →
  5. Verify Registration: All contractors must be registered with the City.
    Contractor Registration →

During & After

  • Ensure all required inspections are completed at each stage.
  • Submit all required inspection reports to your insurance adjuster.
  • Once complete, the City inspector issues a final inspection report - send a copy to your insurance adjuster to close the claim.
Inspections Checklist (PDF) →
⚠️ Avoid Fraud

Never pay for a full job upfront. Final payment should only occur after the City inspector issues the final report.
